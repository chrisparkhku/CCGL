Two things to take back to your team.

◎

Keep your standards high. Make it possible to reach them.

Tell people what good work looks like. Let them suggest better ways to do it. When they struggle, ask what is blocking them.

When people are afraid“I’d better hide this.”
When people can ask for help“Can we work this out?”

Psychological safety means being able to ask questions, admit mistakes and raise concerns. It does not mean that poor work has no consequences.

☷

When someone brings bad news, make the next step useful.

Thank them for telling you. Find out who could be affected. Decide who will fix it, who will help, and when you will check again.

A rise in reported problems can mean people are speaking more honestly. Look at whether problems get solved, too.

FordWhy an honest red report can be useful

Elizabeth Haas Edersheim recounts how Alan Mulally responded when Mark Fields disclosed a Ford Edge launch problem. The story reports more red indicators at the following meeting. It does not establish that one response caused Ford’s wider turnaround.

Edersheim (2016), “Alan Mulally, Ford, and the 6Cs,” Brookings
Psychological safetySpeaking up and maintaining standards

Amy Edmondson explains that accountability and psychological safety are separate dimensions. A team can be expected to do excellent work and still feel able to discuss mistakes or uncertainty.

Amy Edmondson interview, Harvard Business School
Learning from errorsDiscussing, investigating and correcting problems

Van Dyck and colleagues found positive relationships between error management culture and firm performance across two studies. Their concept includes talking about errors and detecting, analysing and correcting them quickly. These findings do not show that every kind of mistake should have the same consequences.

van Dyck, Frese, Baer & Sonnentag (2005), Journal of Applied Psychology, 90(6), 1228–1240
